## The position we'd defend in the room

> **Botany 158 is the available-now, best-spec Grade-A in central Lancashire — with the labour catchment and power capacity the Golden Triangle and Logistics North can't match on cost.**

We lead with the asset and that central-Lancashire labour-and-power wedge because that is what the buying committee is scored on, and occupancy is the only number that's been set. We put **FI's name on the deliverability** — because in a market where power and delivery risk are the fastest-rising occupier fears, a developer that builds and runs its own park is the proof that makes the spec believable. The brand doesn't headline the campaign. It underwrites it.

This is the resolution of a genuine strategic fork we ran adversarially before committing. The full argument is in the appendix; the short version: brand-first marketing on a live disposal optimises the wrong metric and the research files it as "do later." Asset-and-location wins the lead. But FI's in-house build capability is a real letting asset — so we deploy it as *proof*, not positioning.

### Why this position wins in 2026–27 specifically

Three facts from the market, not from us:

1. **NW speculative pipeline is at its lowest since 2019** (~925k sq ft under construction; only four units in the 100–200k band). New best-in-class Grade-A has rental tension behind it.
2. **Lancashire Central / Cuerden is years away** — pre-development as of April 2026, no occupier, no letting agent appointed. That is a clean 2–3 year first-mover window for central Lancashire.
3. **Logistics North and Omega read as "Greater Manchester," not Lancashire.** No NW competitor leads with a structured central-Lancashire labour-catchment narrative. The gap is in the market, not in your logo.

The honest caveat we'd give you up front: Botany 158 sits in the most-contested size band (100–200k sq ft is 68% of NW vacant unit count), and you cannot beat the Golden Triangle on national population reach. That's exactly why the lead is labour-and-power cost, not drive-time bragging.

## Who we target (account-based, not audience-based)

The market for a 158k sq ft shed is finite and nameable. We don't "prospect an audience" — we build a target list and work it.

**Occupier profiles:** 3PLs expanding NW capacity or chasing new contracts · e-commerce/retail fulfilment needing the M6/M61 corridor · UK & inbound manufacturers needing power (up to 550kVA) and 12m clear height · any occupier facing a lease event in the next 12–18 months within ~30 miles.

**The decision unit (a 4–7 person black box — we cover all of it):** Property/Estates Director (sponsor) · Supply-Chain/Operations Director · CFO · Head of HR (labour catchment) · Head of Sustainability (the EPC A on the slide) · and the **tenant-rep agent who builds the shortlist**. At this size, ~60%+ of converted leads come through tenant-rep channels. The agent is a channel to amplify, never a competitor to cut across.

**Hard first action:** confirm and align with FI's disposal-agent mandate (JLL + Trevor Dawson + Box 4 + your in-house team) before any direct demand-gen fires. We feed their pipeline; we don't compete with it.

## The message — what goes above the fold

The buying committee is a black box, so we anchor on facts that survive any reader, in the order the 2025–26 occupier surveys say they're scored on:

1. **Power & grid** — up to 550kVA, EV-charging readiness. (Rated "critical" and the fastest-rising location filter.)
2. **ESG** — BREEAM Very Good, EPC A. (88% of occupiers now rate ESG regulation important, up from 69% in 2024.)
3. **Labour** — Chorley's 2.6% claimant count vs UK 3.8% / NW 3.9%; a mobile, competitively-priced catchment the Golden Triangle can't match on cost.
4. **Spec & access (table-stakes confirmation)** — 158,360 sq ft, 12m clear, 60m yard, 11 dock + 3 level, 136 spaces, M61 J8 in one minute, 56.5% of UK population within a 4.5-hr HGV drive. Necessary, defensible, but *not* the hook — comparable schemes already market 85–90% reach.

**Where FI lives — the guarantor clause, not the headline.** FI Construction's in-house build and the ~1m sq ft Botany Bay masterplan appear precisely at the moment an occupier prices counterparty and delivery risk: *"who stands behind this, can they power it, deliver the fit-out, and still be managing the park in year seven?"* FI is the endorsement on the asset — named alongside the occupiers already on the park (Costa, Greggs, Central Co-op) as social proof. One developer, demonstrably able to build and run the thing. Not a 40-year heritage story.

**The retention thread (what makes this strategic, not an advert).** The bespoke fit-out is the retention engine. A 3PL or manufacturer that builds racking, MHE, power and process flow around this unit faces brutal switching costs at the break. We sell that as a benefit — *"a space built around how you actually run"* — and FI's long-term backing is the proof the relationship lasts. **Occupancy is won at renewal, not just at signing.**

## How we work — solving your actual pain point

Your incumbents are slow to get back to you. On this account that is the one thing we will not be. Concretely:

- **Agents own** occupier outreach, tenant-rep relationships, and negotiation. **We own** brand, content, microsite, PR, LinkedIn and events. Shared asset library, co-branded brochures, white-labelled microsite assets the agents can use in their own outreach.
- **Single lead log.** Every enquiry from an FI/Soap channel routes to the joint agency team through one CRM — agents are never talked past.
- **Monthly enquiry log; quarterly steering meeting.** Headline line is always the same: signed lettings toward 80% occupancy.
- **We respond.** Named contact, fast turnarounds. Given your £30k-per-area and £40–50k-per-property budgets need internal justification, every spend on this campaign ties to a stage in the funnel below.

## How we measure — the only funnel that matters

**Awareness → qualified enquiry → viewing → heads of terms → lease → renewal.**
Reported against occupancy, never impressions in isolation.

Thresholds that would change the plan:

- **Signed HoTs within 6 months** of launch; **signed lease within 12.**
- **No qualified tenant-rep engagement by month 6** → re-test pricing, consider BTS sub-division, raise the incentive package (consistent with Savills' read that incentives are rising and lease lengths shortening as the market turns selective).
- **Labour message not landing** → pivot PR to sector case studies; the James Donaldson Timber Chorley-jobs-retention story is reusable and authentic.

## Appendix — the strategic fork, resolved

We ran the brand-vs-asset decision adversarially before committing, because it's the one genuine fork in this brief.

- **The case for brand-first:** agents triage by developer credibility, and FI's developer brand is "the weakest part of the proposition." Spec-led marketing commoditises you on parity attributes (motorway access, population reach) where you're mid-table.
- **The case for asset-first (which wins the lead):** there is one KPI, and the evidence on what moves a *signed letting* is one-directional — labour, power, ESG, spec, location, fed to a tenant-rep-driven DMU on a clock. The research files developer-brand advertising as Tier 3 / "do later" for unit disposal. You cannot defend a brand-first lead in a room where occupancy is the only number.
- **The synthesis:** the brand-first camp was right that FI's in-house build-and-operate capability is a real letting asset — it's the credible answer to the occupier's fastest-rising fear (power/grid/delivery risk). The mistake was treating it as *brand to communicate* rather than *proof to deploy*. So: lead with the asset and the labour-and-power wedge; let FI show up as the guarantor of deliverability, not a chapter beside the spec. The master-brand transition gets advanced by a fast, professional, well-told disposal — worth more than any amount of Tier-3 corporate advertising, and it costs the occupancy KPI nothing.
